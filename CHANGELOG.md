# Changelog

All notable changes to Thunder are listed here. Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org).
Each release's notes on GitHub are taken from its section below.

## Unreleased

- Runs as a VST3 and CLAP plugin on Windows, macOS and Linux, and as an AU on macOS.
- Every control can be automated in your DAW and is saved with your project. Changes are smoothed, so moving a control or automating it never clicks, and projects saved with earlier versions keep loading.
- Bypassing Thunder in your DAW keeps the track in time and fades smoothly, also on mono tracks.
- **Thunder's own dark interface**:
  - The module chain shows Pitch, Time, Grains, Static, Space, Tone, Width, Shape and Duck in processing order. Click a module to see its controls, or its dot to switch it on or off.
  - Output Mode, Mix, Layer, In and Out, Auto Gain and the limiter are always in view.
  - Drag a knob up or down; hold Shift for fine adjustments. The mouse wheel nudges it, and a double-click resets it.
  - Click a knob's value to type an exact one, or right-click the knob to reset, enter, copy or paste its value.
  - ↺ next to a module's switch resets all of that module's controls.
  - Hover over any control to see what it does, with its value and range.
  - Resize the window from its corner in 5% steps, from 75% to 200%, or pick a size in Settings. Each project remembers its size, and a window that wouldn't fit your screen opens smaller.
  - **Settings** (the gear): window size, the visualiser view new projects open with, the project's **Quality** (Eco uses the least CPU; High has the steadiest pitch on low notes and adds 40 ms of latency), **Reduce motion** (no lightning, flashes or other animation; the waveform, spectrum and meters keep moving; on Windows it starts out following the system's animation setting), the **frame rate** (60 or 30 updates a second; Eco always uses 30), tooltips on or off, capture format and folder, and whether presets keep your Key, Scale and Output Mode.
  - **About** (ⓘ or the logo) shows the version, the plugin format, your system and DAW, and a link for reporting an issue. **Copy details** copies them for your report.
  - Every control gives screen readers its name, what it does and its value.
- **Visualiser**, live while your song plays:
  - **Wave**: your melody (dim) and the layer (glowing) scroll by in time with your song, with its bar numbers and beat lines. Right-click to show 1, 2 or 4 bars, freeze the display, or hide the dry signal.
  - **Grains** appear as sparks over the waveform: further left reads further back, higher is pitched up, violet is panned out. Right-click → Show grains to hide them.
  - **Spectrum**: the layer and your melody from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, with the Tone filter curve on top. Drag its handles to set the high-pass and low-pass. The Tone module has a small version of the curve, and Open in Spectrum.
  - Switch with WAVE | SPECTRUM in the corner. Each project remembers its view; Settings chooses the one new projects open with.
  - When playback stops the picture holds, then dims.
  - **Lightning** strikes from the top of the visualiser onto the layer on its stronger attacks, brighter the louder the output. It adapts to the music: a layer with soft attacks strikes on softer notes, a punchy one only on its bigger hits. **Static's Amount** sets how readily it strikes; with Static off, only strong attacks strike. Never more than three flashes a second, and only the visualiser lights up.
- **Meters** in the Output panel: the level of each channel with peak hold and clip lights (click to clear), the short-term loudness in LUFS, a correlation line that turns amber when parts of the sound would cancel in mono, and a ⚠ while the safety limiter is working.
- **Presets**:
  - 12 built-in presets, plus Init, which resets every control.
  - Click the preset name to open the **browser**: search by name, author, category, bank or tag; filter by category, bank, tag or favourites; sort by any column. Click a preset to hear it, press Enter or double-click to keep it, or Esc to go back to the sound you had. The arrow keys move through the list without loading. Each preset's tags and description are shown.
  - ◄ and ► step through the list as the browser filters it, so you can audition one category. A `*` shows when you've changed the sound.
  - ☆ marks a preset as a favourite.
  - Save your own presets, with a name, author, category, **bank** (a sub-folder), **tags** and description, to `Documents/Thunder/Presets`. Thunder asks before replacing one, and a save never leaves a preset half-written.
  - Rename and delete your presets in the browser. **Import…** adds presets someone sent you without ever replacing one of yours (a different preset with the same name is saved as "Name (2)"); **Export…** saves the current sound as a preset file anywhere. **Open preset folder** and **Rescan** are in the browser too.
  - Files that can't be read are counted, never loaded.
  - The built-in presets also appear in your DAW's own preset menu for Thunder. Reopening a project never loads a preset over your sound.
  - Loading a preset keeps your Key, Scale and Output Mode (you can turn this off in Settings), and switches smoothly while playing.
  - Your project remembers which preset it was on.
- **Morph** (next to the presets): set a sound B, from the current sound or any preset, and the slider moves from your settings (A) to B. Knobs glide in their own way, switches and modes change halfway, and Key, Scale, Output Mode and the safety limiter stay put. While it morphs, each knob shows where Morph has moved it as a thin second ring. Sound B is saved with your project, Morph can be automated, and both ends sound exactly like A and B.
- **Pitch**, in three modes:
  - **Octave:** −2, −1, +1 or +2 octaves (+1 by default).
  - **Semitone:** −24 to +24 semitones, plus Fine (±100 cents).
  - **Interval:** an interval up or down that stays in your key (Key Root and Scale, nine scales). Each note of a single-note melody gets its own in-key interval; chords get the one fixed shift that keeps the most notes in key.
  - Shift and mode changes glide, so switching during playback is click-free.
  - **Preserve Formants** keeps upward shifts from sounding "chipmunky".
  - **Pitch Mix** blends the shifted layer with the unshifted one.
- **Grains**: short, smoothly faded slices of the last few seconds of the layer.
  - **Scale Lock** keeps grain pitches to intervals that stay in your key (fourths, fifths and octaves); **Tempo Sync** starts grains in time with your song; **Freeze** holds the texture for as long as it is on.
  - Size, Density and Amount.
  - Position Spread (reaching up to 4 s back), Pitch Spread (up to ±12 semitones), Pan Spread and Reverse Probability.
  - The grain cloud keeps a steady level however dense or spread out it is.
  - The Grains panel shows the grains playing now: further right reads further back, higher is pitched up, bigger is longer.
- **Space**: four reverbs: lush **Cloud**, bright **Plate**, small **Room** and **Shimmer**, which rises in octaves. Mix, Decay (each with its own range, shown in seconds), Pre-delay and Damping. Switching lets the old reverb ring out.
- **Tone**: a high-pass (on by default, at 200 Hz) and a low-pass on the layer, each 12 or 24 dB/oct.
- **Time**: halftime in step with your song. Each bar plays its first half (1/2) or first quarter (1/4) across the whole bar, at the same pitch and with no added latency. **Smooth** softens the jump at each bar line.
- **Static**: random movement that makes the layer feel alive. It can move how far back the grains reach, their pitch spread, the filters, the pan and the level; choose which with the target buttons.
  - **Smooth** glides, **Stepped** jumps from value to value, **Spark** fires sudden bursts that fade.
  - **Rate** in Hz, or in note values with **Sync**; **Amount** sets how far it moves.
  - The movement follows your song's position, so a loop moves the same way every time and a bounce sounds like playback.
  - The Static panel draws the last two seconds of movement, one line for each target that is on.
- **Shape**: a rhythmic volume shape on the layer: Pump, Swell, Gate, Ramp Up, Ramp Down or Chop, from 2 bars down to 1/16 triplets (dotted and triplet values included). **Depth** sets how deep it cuts, **Smooth** softens its edges. It stays locked to your song's bars and beats through loops and jumps, and keeps time while the transport is stopped. The Shape panel draws one cycle of the shape, with a playhead where your song is in it.
- **Width**: stereo width from mono to 200%. Mono playback sounds the same at every width. The Width panel shows how the output sums to mono. **Autopan** moves the layer from side to side in time with your song, with its own rate and depth.
- **Duck**: turns the layer down while your melody plays, so it stays behind it, and lets it back up with Release. Works in Layer Only too. A meter shows how far it is turning the layer down.
- Every module has its own on/off switch, which fades in and out without clicks.
- **Output**:
  - **Blend** (your melody plus the layer) or **Layer Only**. At the default Mix of 50% both play at full level.
  - **Auto Gain** keeps the layer 6 dB under your melody.
  - **Layer Level**, **Input Gain** and **Output Gain**.
  - A **safety limiter** keeps the output under −0.3 dBFS.
- The layer stays exactly in time with your melody: Thunder reports its latency (about 82 ms) to your DAW, which compensates for it.
- Offline bounces sound exactly like playback.
- **Capture**: record 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars of the layer, of Thunder's whole output, or of its input, to a WAV file and drag it straight into your DAW.
  - Arm it and press play: recording starts at the next bar, and the file starts exactly on that bar.
  - A loop that wraps around on a bar line keeps recording; if playback stops or jumps elsewhere, the capture is cancelled and no file is left behind.
  - 24-bit or 32-bit float at your project's sample rate, saved to `Documents/Thunder/Captures` or a folder you choose, with descriptive names such as `Thunder_A-min_140bpm_4bars_20261121-143005.wav`.
  - Right-click a capture to Save as…, Show in folder or Delete it.
  - Thunder on several tracks can capture at the same time; each capture gets its own file.
  - **Include tail** (in the length menu) keeps recording after the last bar until the reverb and grains have died away, for up to 10 s. Stopping playback doesn't cut the tail short; pressing Stop ends it and keeps the capture.
  - **Loop** folds that tail back onto the start, so the file is exactly the bars you chose and loops seamlessly. If your DAW looped and played the melody again, the start isn't doubled.
- **Key detection**: Thunder listens to your melody and shows the key it hears next to the Key controls, with how sure it is (cyan when confident, amber when unsure). Click it to use the detected key or keep yours; Thunder never changes your key by itself. Turn listening off for a project in the same menu.
- **Source**: drop a WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 or OGG file (up to 5 minutes) anywhere on Thunder, or capture your input, to load it as the source. It appears across the top of the visualiser with its waveform, bar lines and key, and the key badge shows its key. ✕ removes it.
  - Your project remembers the source. Sources up to about half a minute are also saved inside the project, so they come back even if the file moves; if a file can't be found, Thunder says so and offers Locate….
- **Installing**: each download includes step-by-step instructions for your system. On Linux, Thunder runs on Ubuntu 22.04 or newer (glibc 2.35+), on x86_64 and ARM64.
